## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> **Slide 1 · Title**
>
> Good morning, everyone. My name is [name], and I work on occupational dosimetry at a nuclear power research institute. Today I'd like to share a simple question we tried to answer: can the dosimeter on a worker's chest tell us enough about the dose to their eyes?
>
> **Slide 2 · Why this matters**
>
> As many of you know, in 2011 the ICRP lowered the occupational limit for the lens of the eye from 150 to 20 millisieverts per year, averaged over five years. That's a reduction of more than eighty-five percent. In hospitals, this has meant new eye dosimeters for interventional staff. But for nuclear power plants, the picture is less clear. Our fields are dominated by high-energy photons, and in some tasks there's beta radiation as well. So the question is: do we really need dedicated eye dosimeters for everyone?
>
> **Slide 3 · What we did**
>
> To find out, we monitored forty-two workers during one refueling outage. Each worker wore an eye lens dosimeter on a headband, calibrated for Hp(3), together with their usual chest dosimeter. That gave us one hundred and sixty-eight paired measurements across six types of maintenance work.
>
> **Slide 4 · Main result**
>
> Here's the main result. On this plot, each dot is one pair: eye dose on the vertical axis, chest dose on the horizontal axis. As you can see, most of the dots sit very close to the line of equality. The median ratio of Hp(3) to Hp(10) was 1.08. In other words, for most tasks, the chest dosimeter gave a good estimate of the eye dose, just slightly low, by about eight percent on average.
>
> **Slide 5 · The exceptions**
>
> But there were exceptions, and they're interesting. In six pairs, the eye dose was more than one and a half times the chest dose. All six came from valve maintenance near contaminated surfaces. And in all six, the shallow dose, Hp(0.07), was also high. This points to beta radiation, or possibly low-energy photons, that the chest dosimeter simply doesn't see. I should stress that we didn't measure the spectrum, so this is our best interpretation, not a proven cause.
>
> **Slide 6 · What this means**
>
> So what does this mean in practice? We think a graded approach makes sense. For most tasks, the routine chest dosimeter is enough, as long as doses stay well below the limit. For work close to contaminated components, we recommend dedicated eye dosimeters.
>
> **Slide 7 · Limitations and close**
>
> Of course, this is one outage at one plant, so we need more data, especially for contractors who work at several units in a row. Thank you very much for your attention. I'm happy to take any questions.

저희는 차등적 접근이 타당하다고 봅니다.
어휘: make sense 타당하다

**㉖ For most tasks, the routine chest dosimeter is enough, as long as doses stay well below the limit.**
대부분의 작업은 선량이 한도보다 훨씬 낮은 한 평소의 가슴 선량계로 충분합니다.

**㉗ For work close to contaminated components, we recommend dedicated eye dosimeters.**
오염된 기기 가까이에서 하는 작업에는 전용 수정체 선량계를 권합니다.

### 문단 7 · Slide 7 · Limitations and close

**㉘ Of course, this is one outage at one plant, so we need more data, especially for contractors who work at several units in a row.**
물론 이것은 한 발전소의 한 번 정비 기간 자료이므로 더 많은 자료가 필요합니다. 특히 여러 호기를 연이어 오가며 일하는 협력업체 작업자에 대해서요.
어휘: in a row 연이어

**㉙ Thank you very much for your attention.**
경청해 주셔서 감사합니다.

**㉚ I'm happy to take any questions.**
질문 있으시면 받겠습니다.

## 학술 영어 포인트

### 1. 같은 내용, 문어와 구어

| 논문 (D10, 문어) | 발표 원고 (D16, 구어) | 차이 |
|---|---|---|
| This study found that, for most maintenance tasks…, lens doses estimated from trunk-worn dosimeters calibrated in Hp(10) were close to Hp(3) measured near the eyes. | can the dosimeter on a worker's chest tell us enough about the dose to their eyes? | 결론 진술 → 청중에게 던지는 질문 |
| All six pairs with a ratio above 1.5 involved valve maintenance near contaminated surfaces | All six came from valve maintenance near contaminated surfaces. | 문장 짧게, 동사 단순하게 |
| this explanation should be regarded as provisional | I should stress that we didn't measure the spectrum, so this is our best interpretation, not a proven cause. | 완곡 표현 → 1인칭 말하기 |
| routine whole-body dosimetry appears to provide an adequate basis… | For most tasks, the routine chest dosimeter is enough, as long as doses stay well below the limit. | 학술 어휘 → 일상 어휘 (trunk-worn → chest) |
| A graded approach… may therefore offer a reasonable balance… | We think a graded approach makes sense. | 명사구 → 짧은 주장 |

구어에서는 축약형(I'd, That's, there's, doesn't)이 자연스럽고, 문장 하나에 정보 하나를 담습니다. 청중은 다시 읽을 수 없기 때문입니다(U29).

### 2. 발표의 길잡이 표현 (signposting)

| 기능 | 표현 |
|---|---|
| 주제 소개 | Today I'd like to share a simple question we tried to answer |
| 공유 지식 환기 | As many of you know, … |
| 문제 제기 | So the question is: … |
| 방법 전환 | To find out, … |
| 결과 제시 | Here's the main result. / On this plot, … / As you can see, … |
| 재진술 | In other words, … |
| 반전 | But there were exceptions, and they're interesting. |
| 함의로 전환 | So what does this mean in practice? |
| 마무리 | Thank you very much for your attention. I'm happy to take any questions. |

### 3. 예상 질문과 답변 연습 (3단계 연결)

| 예상 질문 | 답변 예시 |
|---|---|
| Why didn't you measure the energy spectrum? | That's a fair point. Spectrometry wasn't feasible during the outage, so we relied on the Hp(0.07) ratio as an indirect indicator. It's something we'd like to add in the next campaign. |
| Was the 8% underestimation consistent across tasks? | Not entirely. It varied with geometry, mainly the worker's posture and where the source was. The detailed breakdown by task is in the paper, and I'm happy to share it. |
| Would you recommend Hp(3) dosimeters for contractors? | For contractors who move between units, yes, at least for tasks near contaminated components, because their annual doses can accumulate. But I'd base that on dose records rather than a blanket rule. |

답변 구조는 ① 질문 인정(That's a fair point / Not entirely) → ② 간결한 사실 → ③ 한계나 다음 단계입니다. 모르는 질문에는 "I don't have that data with me, but I'd be glad to follow up after the session."처럼 정직하게 답합니다.
